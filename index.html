How to use the calculator

  1. Pick filament or resin. Add a row for each colour or material in the part, with its price and weight.
  2. Enter the weight (or resin volume) and print time that your slicer shows.
  3. Add hands-on time, hardware and packaging. For a batch, set the units, setup time and failure rate.
  4. Open printer and energy settings if your printer price, power draw or electricity rate is different.
  5. Pick a margin, add your GST rate, and save the quote.

How the cost is worked out

Material
for each filament, weight (g) ÷ 1000 × price per kg; add them up, then × waste factor
Machine and energy
print hours × (depreciation + maintenance + electricity per hour) × downtime buffer
Depreciation per hour
(printer price + upfront cost) ÷ (lifespan in years × 8,760 h × uptime)
Electricity per hour
power (W) ÷ 1000 × price per kWh
Labor
hands-on minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate
Total cost
material + machine + labor + hardware + packaging (+ resin consumables)
Batch cost per unit
material, machine and consumables ÷ (1 − failure rate), plus setup labor ÷ units
Selling price
total cost ÷ (1 − margin), then × (1 + GST rate)

Questions and answers

How much does it cost to 3D print something?

It depends on the material, the print time and your labor. The worked example above, a 50 g PLA part that prints in 3 hours, costs about ₹135 to make with the default settings.

Where do I find the weight and print time?

Slice the model first. Most slicers, including Cura, PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio, show the estimated filament weight and print time on the result screen.

What do the waste factor and buffer do?

The waste factor adds material for failed prints, purge and supports (1.1 means 10% extra). The buffer raises machine cost to cover downtime and slow days.

How is electricity calculated?

Power draw in watts ÷ 1000 × your price per kWh, for each hour of printing. A 150 W printer at ₹8 per kWh uses about ₹1.20 of electricity per hour.

How much should I charge?

Start with your total cost and add a margin. Margin here is the share of the selling price that is profit, so 40% margin is not the same as a 40% markup. Leave room for failed prints, customer support and your time.

How do I price a multi-colour print?

Add one filament row for each colour or material, with the weight your slicer shows for it. The calculator adds them up. Include purge and tower waste in the weights or raise the waste factor.

How does batch pricing work?

Setup time (slicing, bed prep, loading) happens once per batch, so it is split across all units. The failure rate adds the cost of reprints. The Price by quantity table shows how the price per unit changes with run size.

Does it work for resin printing?

Yes. Switch to Resin, enter the volume in mL and your price per liter, and add a consumables cost for IPA, gloves and FEP wear. The waste factor starts at 1.2.

How does GST work in the price?

Enter your GST rate and the price shown includes it. Your profit is calculated before tax, because GST is collected for the government and is not part of your margin.
